How to check a photo’s size on iPhone

By Mohamed Elatabany · Updated 15 November 2026

Open the photo in Photos and tap the info button (ⓘ) or swipe up: the panel shows its size in pixels, such as 3024 × 4032. A photo has no fixed size in inches until you print it. Divide the pixels by 300 for the largest sharp print: 10.08 × 13.44 in (25.6 × 34.1 cm) for a 3024 × 4032 photo.

“Size” can mean three different things for a photo: its pixels, its file size, and how big it comes out on paper. This guide shows where to find each one and how to turn pixels into a print size you can use.

1. See the pixel size on iPhone or iPad

  1. Open the Photos app and tap the photo.
  2. Tap the info button (ⓘ) below the photo, or swipe up on it.
  3. Read the line under the camera name: the megapixels, the width × height in pixels and the file size, for example 12 MP · 3024 × 4032 · 2.4 MB.

Width comes first. A photo taken upright is taller than it is wide, so 3024 × 4032 is a portrait photo and 4032 × 3024 the same photo sideways. Screenshots, downloads and photos saved from messaging apps show their own pixel size in the same place, often much smaller than a camera photo. Apple describes the info panel in its iPhone User Guide.

For printing, what counts is the part of the photo you will print: cropping keeps fewer pixels than the panel shows for the whole photo.

2. Pixels, file size and print size

Three meanings of “photo size”
SizeExampleWhat it tells you
Pixels3024 × 4032How much detail the photo holds, and so how large it can print sharply.
File size2.4 MBHow much storage it takes. It changes with compression and format, so it says little about print size.
Print size4 × 6 inThe size on paper. You choose it when you print; the pixels decide how sharp it looks at that size.

3. Turn pixels into inches or cm

Divide each pixel dimension by the pixels per inch (PPI) you want on paper. 300 PPI looks sharp even up close; 200 PPI still looks good at normal viewing distance. Multiply inches by 2.54 for centimetres.

Worked example: 3024 ÷ 300 = 10.08 and 4032 ÷ 300 = 13.44, so a 12 MP iPhone photo prints sharp up to 10.08 × 13.44 in, or 25.6 × 34.1 cm. Anything smaller, such as a 4 × 6 or 5 × 7, has detail to spare.

Cropping to a different shape uses fewer pixels: a 4 × 6 print cuts about 11% off an iPhone photo. To check a particular photo at a particular size, choose it in the print quality checker, which reads the pixel size in your browser and shows the PPI after the crop. To convert sizes without a photo, use the photo size calculator.

4. Why it says 72 DPI

Many photos, including those from iPhone, carry a resolution tag of 72 pixels per inch. Some programs divide the pixels by that tag and report a 3024 × 4032 photo as 42 × 56 inches. That figure is not a print size and not a quality limit. The tag is only a note in the file: when you print, the size you choose sets how many pixels land on each inch, and the photo's pixels decide whether that looks sharp.

5. Check the size on a Mac or Windows PC

  • Photos on Mac: select the photo and click the Info button in the toolbar, or press Command-I. The Info window lists the pixel dimensions and file size.
  • Preview on Mac: open the image and click the Info button in the toolbar. In the Inspector, General Info shows the image size in pixels (Apple’s Preview guide).
  • Finder: select the file, choose File › Get Info, and look under More Info for Dimensions.
  • Windows: right-click the file in File Explorer, choose Properties, open the Details tab and read Dimensions.

6. Measure a printed photo

For a photo that is already printed, measure the paper, not the picture area, with a ruler in millimetres. Common sizes are 51 × 51 mm (2 × 2 in), 35 × 45 mm (passport and visa photos), 51 × 76 mm or 64 × 89 mm (wallet photos), 102 × 152 mm (4 × 6 in, often sold as 10 × 15 cm), 127 × 178 mm (5 × 7 in) and 203 × 254 mm (8 × 10 in). A print that is a few millimetres short of one of these was usually scaled by the printer: see why prints come out the wrong size.

7. See the real size before you print

Numbers on a screen are hard to picture. In Photo Print, set the print size you want in inches, cm or mm, then tap the photo in your layout to open it:

  • Print quality shows the PPI and a rating for that size, and the pixels the crop uses.
  • How large can it print? lists the largest size at Excellent (300 PPI) and Good (200 PPI) quality, in your chosen unit.
  • See actual size shows the photo on the screen at the exact size it will print, with rulers, on iPhones and iPads whose screen size the app knows. Hold a printed photo against it to compare.

The app never shrinks a photo to fit: if a size is too big for the paper, it tells you instead.

Photo size questions

How do I see the pixel size of a photo on iPhone?

Open the photo in the Photos app, then tap the info button (ⓘ) or swipe up. The details panel shows the megapixels, the width × height in pixels and the file size.

How big is my photo in inches?

A digital photo has no fixed size in inches. Divide each pixel dimension by 300 to get the largest size that prints sharp, or by 200 for a size that still looks good in a frame. A 3024 × 4032 photo is 10.08 × 13.44 in at 300 PPI.

Why does my iPhone photo say 72 DPI?

That number is a tag stored in the file. It does not limit the print size or the quality. The printed size is the size you choose when you print.

Does the file size in MB show how big I can print?

No. File size depends on compression and the image format. The pixel dimensions decide how large a photo prints well.

How can I see how big a photo will be before printing it?

Set the print size in Photo Print and tap See actual size to view the photo at that size on the screen, with rulers. Or print a test sheet at 100% and measure it.
