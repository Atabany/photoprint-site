How the count is worked out

Photos are placed left to right along a row, and rows go down the page, the way you would lay prints on a table. Each photo keeps its exact size. When turning is allowed, it also tries photos lying sideways and keeps whichever arrangement holds more. It tries both directions of paper too.

The margin is taken off every edge first. What is left is the printable area, and nothing may cross it. If a single photo is larger than that area, the calculator says so instead of shrinking it, because a 2 × 2 photo printed smaller is no longer a 2 × 2 photo.

Turn the paper, not the faces

A 4 × 6 sheet is 101.6 × 152.4 mm. In portrait, three 35 × 45 mm photos stack down the 152.4 mm side and two sit across the 101.6 mm side: six photos. Turned to landscape, four fit across the long side (140 mm) and two down the short side (90 mm): eight photos, and every face is still upright. Larger paper gains too: 32 on landscape A4 against 30 in portrait, and 28 on landscape US Letter against 25.

Margins: photo paper and plain paper

Photo paper in 4 × 6 and 5 × 7 is usually printed borderless, so the whole sheet is usable. On A4 and Letter most home printers cannot print right to the edge; Photo Print uses a ¼ in (6.35 mm) margin there by default. If your printer's margins are larger, choose a bigger margin above. The printer test sheet shows how close to the edge your printer prints.

Gaps and cutting

With no gap, two neighbouring photos share one cut line, so one straight cut separates them. A gap of 2–3 mm is forgiving if you cut with scissors, at the cost of fewer photos per sheet. Either way, print cut guides and use a ruler or a paper trimmer.

Print the sheet at actual size

A planned sheet only comes out right when it is printed at 100%. Choose Actual Size or 100% scale, turn off Fit to Page, and measure one photo with a ruler. If it is the wrong size, see why prints come out the wrong size.

Frequently asked questions

How many 2 × 2 photos fit on a 4 × 6?

Six, in three rows of two, on borderless 4 × 6 in paper with no gap between them. With a 3 mm gap only two fit, because 2 + 2 inches plus a gap is wider than the 4 inch sheet.

How many passport photos fit on a 4 × 6 sheet?

Six 2 × 2 in photos. For 35 × 45 mm photos, 6 fit on a portrait sheet and 8 when the sheet is turned to landscape, with every face still upright.

How many wallet photos fit on a page?

Four 2 × 3 in wallets fit on a 4 × 6 print edge to edge; with a 3 mm gap only 2 fit. A US Letter page with ¼ in margins holds 13 with the page turned to landscape (12 in portrait). The larger 2.5 × 3.5 in wallet fits 2 on a 4 × 6 and 9 on Letter.

How many 4 × 6 photos fit on a Letter or A4 page?

Three on US Letter with no gap (two upright and one sideways below them), or two with gaps. A4 holds two. These counts use ¼ in margins.

Is the count the same in the Photo Print app?

Yes, with the same margin and space settings. The calculator uses the same layout rules as Photo Print, and its Automatic settings are the app’s defaults: no space between ID photos, 3 mm between everyday prints. For edge-to-edge wallets, set Space between photos to 0 in the app. The app makes up to 50 copies of one photo at a time; add the photo again to go beyond that.

Does this calculator need my photos?

No. It only needs the photo size and the paper, and it runs entirely in your browser.
