How to print a photo at a specific size on iPhone

By Mohamed Elatabany · Updated 4 November 2026

The iPhone Photos app has no setting for the printed width and height. It places the photo on the paper you choose. To print a photo at an exact size, such as 5 × 7 in, 8 × 10 in or 13 × 18 cm, set that size in a layout app or a PDF, then print the page at 100% / Actual size and check it with a ruler.

This guide covers when the built-in route is enough, how to enter any width and height in Photo Print, which sizes fit on A4 and US Letter, and how to avoid a print that comes out cropped or scaled.

1. When the Photos app is enough

Apple’s steps are Photos → open the photo → Share → Print, with any AirPrint printer. The guide does not describe a print-size field. The options shown depend on your printer, and usually include the printer, copies and sometimes the paper size.

That works when the print size is the paper size. If you load 4 × 6 or 5 × 7 photo paper and your printer prints borderless, choose that paper and the photo fills it. For anything else, such as a 5 × 7 on A4, two 4 × 6 prints on Letter or a 13 × 18 cm print, the printer has no way to know the size you want. The 4 × 6 guide explains the paper-matching route in detail.

2. Set an exact size in Photo Print

  1. If you measure in centimeters or millimeters, open Settings → Unit and choose it. Sizes then show and accept that unit.
  2. Tap New Layout. Under Photo size, choose Standard (4 × 6 in), Large (5 × 7 in), Wallet (2 × 3 in) or Custom and type the Width and Height.
  3. Under Paper, choose the sheet in your printer: A4, US Letter or 4 × 6.
  4. Under How photos fit, choose Fill to cover the whole print and crop the edges, or Fit to keep the whole photo with blank space.
  5. Add your photos. To change one later, open it. Under Print size you can pick a preset, type a new width and height, use Swap width & height or set Copies.
  6. Check the preview, then use Print, Save PDF or Share PDF. With no printer, use No printer? Use a photo lab to get a 4 × 6 inch image for a lab.

Photo Print never shrinks a photo to fit the paper. If a size is too large for the printable area, the photo is left off and the app shows the largest size that fits. You can then choose a smaller size, a smaller margin or larger paper. It may turn a photo 90° to fit it on the page, but it does not change the size.

3. Common sizes, pixels and paper fit

The fit columns use Photo Print’s default ¼ inch (6.35 mm) page margin. That leaves 197.3 × 284.3 mm on A4 and 203.2 × 266.7 mm (8 × 10.5 in) on US Letter. A photo may be turned sideways to fit.

Pixels are each side in inches × 300, rounded up (divide millimeters by 25.4 first). They are a target for the visible crop, not a hard minimum. The app rates each photo’s print quality from its pixels and crop. Use the photo size calculator for any other size, and the multiple-photos guide to see how many copies of one size fit on a page.

4 × 6 inches and 10 × 15 cm are not quite the same.

4 × 6 in is 101.6 × 152.4 mm. Photo paper sold as “10 × 15 cm” is usually that same 4 × 6 in sheet, but a print set to exactly 100 × 150 mm is 1.6 mm narrower and 2.4 mm shorter. Enter the size your frame or form actually asks for.

4. Why the photo gets cropped

An iPhone photo in the standard 4:3 format rarely has the same shape as the print. 4 × 6 is 2:3, 5 × 7 is 5:7 and 8 × 10 is 4:5. The size stays exact either way. What changes is the content:

  • Fill scales the photo to cover the whole rectangle and crops whatever falls outside. Drag and zoom to choose which part stays. This is right for frames and albums.
  • Fit keeps the whole photo inside the rectangle and leaves blank bands on two sides. Use it when nothing may be cut off, such as a document or artwork.

Typing a different width or height never stretches the photo. The crop changes instead.

5. Worked example: 8 × 10 on US Letter

Suppose you want an 8 × 10 inch portrait of a 12-megapixel iPhone photo, 3024 × 4032 pixels when upright, printed on US Letter.

  • Paper fit: Letter is 8.5 × 11 in. With a ¼ inch margin, the printable area is exactly 8 × 10.5 in, so an 8 × 10 print fits with 0.5 in to spare in height. On A4 (210 mm wide) the 203.2 mm width fits only if each margin is 3.4 mm or less.
  • Crop: the photo is 3:4 (0.75) and the print is 4:5 (0.8), so the photo is relatively taller. With Fill, the full 3024-pixel width is used and the visible height is 3024 ÷ 0.8 = 3780 pixels. That crops 252 pixels, or 6.25% of the height, which you can take from the top, the bottom or both.
  • Sharpness: 3024 pixels across 8 inches is 378 PPI, above the 300 PPI target of 2400 × 3000.

After printing, measure the photo. It should be 203.2 mm wide and 254 mm tall.

6. Print at 100% and measure

With AirPrint from the app, choose the printer and confirm the paper matches the loaded sheet. Photo Print sends the page at its real size. If you print the PDF from another app or a computer, choose Actual size or 100% and turn off Fit to page. Adobe documents that Actual size prints without scaling, while Fit resizes the page to the printable area.

Measure one edge with a ruler. If a 254 mm side measures 244 mm, the output was scaled to about 96%. Fix the print setting rather than entering a bigger size to compensate. The free printer test sheet shows your printer’s scaling and margins on plain paper. A print shop needs the same instruction: print the PDF at 100% on the matching paper. Questions

Can the iPhone Photos app print a photo at a specific size?

Not directly. Apple’s steps are Photos → Share → Print, and the photo is placed on the paper you choose. There is no field for a printed width and height, so set the size in a layout app or a PDF and print that at 100%.

Can I print an 8 × 10 photo on A4 paper?

Only with very small margins. An 8 × 10 inch photo is 203.2 mm wide and A4 is 210 mm wide, so each side margin must be 3.4 mm or less, and many printers cannot print that close to the edge. US Letter fits 8 × 10 with a ¼ inch margin.

Why is my printed photo cropped?

The photo’s shape differs from the print size. iPhone photos are usually 4:3, while 4 × 6 is 2:3, 5 × 7 is 5:7 and 8 × 10 is 4:5. Fill covers the whole print and crops the extra; Fit keeps the whole photo and leaves blank space.

How many pixels do I need for a specific print size?

Multiply each side in inches by 300 for a sharp print, or divide millimeters by 25.4 first. A 5 × 7 inch print needs about 1500 × 2100 pixels in the visible crop.
