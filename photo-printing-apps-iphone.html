iPhone Photo Printing Apps: Compare Four Workflows

By Mohamed Elatabany · Updated 18 October 2026

Use Apple Photos with AirPrint for a straightforward photo print, Photo Print for measured photo sheets and portable layouts, or your printer maker’s app for supported printer setup and controls. The best choice depends on your paper, required dimensions and printer model, rather than a single app winning every task.

This comparison is written by Mohamed Elatabany, the developer of Photo Print. It compares the official documentation linked below with Photo Print’s implementation, checked on 18 October 2026. It is not an independent hands-on test of every printer. Features not established by those sources remain unverified.

Start with Apple Photos for a simple print

For one ordinary photo, try the system workflow first. Open the photo, use Share or Actions, select Print, choose your printer and review the available options. Apple documents the AirPrint network and compatibility requirements. The options you see depend on the selected printer; check its paper settings rather than assuming the panel will look identical on another model.

This is a useful first test because it separates printer connectivity from layout design. If the printer does not appear here, installing a layout app will not automatically make unsupported hardware compatible with AirPrint. Resolve the network or model question before preparing a complicated sheet.

When the job is simply a photo on the loaded photo paper and the available options meet your needs, this workflow may be enough. If you need several independently sized rectangles on a larger sheet, prepare that layout explicitly and check its measurements instead of repeatedly changing a preview until it looks close.

Choose Photo Print for dimensions and reusable sheets

Photo Print’s focus is the physical layout: choose inches, centimetres or millimetres, set the photo size, add copies or different images, and preview them on A4, US Letter or 4 × 6 paper. Fit preserves the whole image inside the rectangle; Fill covers the rectangle and may crop the image. Inspect important details at the edges before output.

The app reports photos that do not fit the configured printable area rather than silently shrinking them. Its PDF output preserves the page geometry, and direct printing uses AirPrint. It also exports a JPEG sheet for a photo-lab workflow. These outputs serve different destinations: ask the shop which file type and paper size it accepts.

The first completed output and three layouts are included. Optional Pro is a one-time purchase for further output and additional capabilities. Check the current options in the app. Photo Print is a layout tool, not an online printing service, and it does not certify a passport or identity photograph’s acceptance.

Choose Canon PRINT when the printer is the main question

Canon’s manual describes wireless smartphone photo printing and guided printer Wi-Fi setup through Canon PRINT. For a supported Canon model, that is a relevant place to start when you need the manufacturer’s connection workflow. Follow the instructions for your particular printer rather than assuming every Canon product supports the same steps.

This guide does not mark exact-size layouts, PDF output or a particular advanced control as absent merely because Canon’s introductory page does not establish it. Check the model’s manual for the feature you need. A layout app and a manufacturer app can also complement one another: one prepares a sheet while the other helps with supported hardware operations.

Choose Epson Smart Panel for a supported Epson device

Epson documents Smart Panel for selected compatible models, including setup, photo and document printing, scanning, copying, status information and troubleshooting. Its photo-printing instructions include paper size, paper type, copies and border choices. These are printer-specific tasks that can matter more than a custom layout when you are getting a new device working.

Check Epson’s compatibility list first. Do not assume Smart Panel supports an older printer because another Epson app supports it; Epson may direct that model to iPrint or another workflow. Scanning and copying are separate needs from arranging measured photo sheets, so choose based on the whole job you need to complete.

Use the same physical test whichever app you choose

Prepare one sheet, print it on the specified paper and measure the output with a ruler. A 2 × 2 inch rectangle should measure 50.8 × 50.8 mm. If the result is smaller, inspect scaling; if edges are missing, inspect the printable area. Changing the requested dimensions to compensate can hide a setting that will affect the next job too.

For PDFs, choose Actual size or 100% where the printing software offers that setting, and match the physical paper to the file’s page size. Borderless enlargement and printer margins can still affect the result. Use the free printer test sheet, the size calculator and the PDF print checklist to diagnose a mismatch.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a paid app just to print one photo?

Try Apple Photos and AirPrint first if your printer supports them. A separate layout app is useful when you need controls or a sheet arrangement that your current workflow does not provide.

Can Photo Print make any printer work with AirPrint?

No. Direct printing requires compatible hardware. Export a PDF or photo-lab JPEG when you need another supported printing route.

Which workflow guarantees an exact final print?

None can guarantee every printer setting. Prepare the correct dimensions, match the paper, check scaling and measure the first sheet.
