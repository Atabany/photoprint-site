Print passport photos at a photo lab — no printer needed

By Mohamed Elatabany · Updated 29 October 2026

Make the sheet in Photo Print, save it as a 4 × 6 inch photo sheet (101.6 × 152.4 mm) — 1200 × 1800 pixels at 300 pixels per inch — and order an ordinary 4 × 6 print at a store photo kiosk or online lab. Ask for no border and turn off auto-crop and auto-fix, then cut along the guides.

Size is only one requirement.

Check the issuing authority’s current rules for the portrait itself — background, expression, lighting and age of the photo. Photo Print prints the exact size you choose; it does not check a photo against any government’s requirements, and some offices take the photo themselves.

Why a 4 × 6 photo

Photo Print exports a 4 × 6 inch sheet for a photo lab or store kiosk. The file is 1200 × 1800 pixels at 300 PPI. Choose a matching print size and turn off cropping or resizing where the service offers those controls, then check a physical proof. A service’s size label alone does not establish the final printed dimensions.

Is 4 × 6 inches the same as 10 × 15 cm?

Not as exact measurements: 4 × 6 inches = 101.6 × 152.4 mm, while a literal 10 × 15 cm = 100 × 150 mm. Both have the same 2:3 aspect ratio, so the preview can look right even when every photo prints slightly smaller.

“10 × 15 cm” can also be a rounded product name for 4 × 6 inch paper. For example, Canon’s paper-size list labels a size “4"x6" 10x15cm” but specifies 10.16 × 15.24 cm. Ask your lab which physical size it produces; do not assume every service uses the same convention.

Worked example: if the full 4 × 6 sheet is resized uniformly onto exactly 100 × 150 mm, its scale is 100 ÷ 101.6 ≈ 98.43%. A 2 × 2 inch photo (50.8 × 50.8 mm) then measures 50 × 50 mm, not 50.8 mm. This is a size-conversion example, not a claim about a particular lab or an official tolerance.

Keep the intended photo dimensions in the app. First check the lab’s physical print size, border and crop settings rather than enlarging the portrait to compensate for an unexplained mismatch. Troubleshoot a wrong-size print if a measured proof differs.

Make the sheet

  1. On the Home screen, tap ID photos and choose your size, for example 2 × 2 in or 35 × 45 mm.
  2. Pick your portrait. Photo Print fills a 4 × 6 sheet with copies and cut guides.
  3. From version 1.6, Photo Print frames the face for you: it finds the face on your iPhone and sizes and centres the head for the size’s head guide. Check it, and move or zoom if you need to. The head goes between the guide’s outlines. You can set the background to white or light grey. Not for US or UK passports: the US State Department and HM Passport Office reject photos altered by software, so take those against a plain white or off-white wall and keep the original.
  4. On the Export tab, use No printer? Use a photo lab to save the sheet as a photo. Save it to Photos, or share it straight to a lab’s app or website.

Check the first print

Measure one photo with a ruler before cutting: a 2 × 2 in photo is 50.8 mm on each side, a 35 × 45 mm photo is exactly that. If it is smaller, the lab fitted or bordered the image — ask them to print it again at 4 × 6 without a border or cropping.

How many photos per print

A 4 × 6 sheet holds six 2 × 2 in or six 35 × 45 mm photos, four 50 × 60 mm photos, or nine 30 × 40 mm photos, with cut guides between them. One inexpensive 4 × 6 print usually covers an application with copies to spare.

Photo-lab questions

Do I need my own printer?

No. Export a 4 × 6 inch JPEG sheet, then order a matching print from a lab or kiosk. The lab handles the order and charges separately; Photo Print creates the file.

Why did a 2 × 2 inch photo print as 50 × 50 mm?

One possible cause is resizing the full 4 × 6 inch sheet to exactly 100 × 150 mm. That scales a 50.8 mm photo to 50 mm. Check the lab’s actual paper size, borders and cropping, and measure a proof.

Is the photo-lab export included with the free download?

Your first completed print, PDF or photo-lab output is included. Further outputs need Pro, as a weekly or yearly subscription or a one-time lifetime unlock. The separate digital US passport photo export is Pro only; it is not the lab sheet.
