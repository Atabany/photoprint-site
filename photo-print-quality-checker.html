Photo print quality checker

By Mohamed Elatabany · Updated 15 November 2026

A photo print looks sharp at 300 pixels per inch (PPI) and good at 200 PPI; below 150 PPI it starts to look soft. Choose a photo and a print size: the checker crops it the way the print will, then shows the PPI, what gets cut off and the largest size that stays sharp. The photo is opened in your browser and never uploaded.

Screenshots and resized copies

Problems usually come from files with fewer pixels than the camera made. A 1170 × 2532 iPhone screenshot prints a 4 × 6 at 293 PPI (Good), but the crop removes 30.7% of it, and at 8 × 10 it drops to 146 PPI (Low). A copy resized to 1600 × 1200 pixels, as some messaging and social apps do, gives 267 PPI at 4 × 6, 150 at 8 × 10 and 109 at 11 × 14. When you can, print from the original in your photo library.

Why cropping lowers the PPI

The checker counts only the pixels that reach the paper. When a photo is cropped to fill a print of a different shape, the cut-off part takes its pixels with it, and zooming in on part of a photo cuts more. That is why the result names the pixels the print uses, not the whole photo. See pixels, PPI and cropping for worked examples.

Fill or fit?

Fill covers the whole print and crops whatever sticks out, which is what photo labs do by default. Fit keeps every part of the photo and leaves blank paper on two sides. ID photos are always filled and kept upright. The crop calculator shows what each print size cuts off.

Find a photo's pixel size

  • iPhone or iPad: open the photo in Photos and swipe up, or tap the info button. The size appears as, for example, 3024 × 4032.
  • Mac: open the photo in Preview and choose Tools › Show Inspector.
  • Windows: right-click the file, choose Properties, then Details, and read Dimensions.

Frequently asked questions

What resolution do I need for a 4 × 6 print?

1200 × 1800 pixels gives 300 PPI, which is sharp even up close. 800 × 1200 pixels gives 200 PPI, which looks sharp at normal viewing distance. Count only the pixels left after cropping.

Is 150 PPI good enough?

For a large print seen from across a room, often yes. Up close it looks a little soft. Below 150 PPI most photos look visibly soft or pixelated.

Will an iPhone photo print sharp at 8 × 10?

Yes. A 12 MP photo (3024 × 4032) cropped to 8 × 10 still has 378 PPI. Even 11 × 14 is rated Good. Detail runs out around 16 × 20, where it drops to Fair.

Is my photo uploaded?

No. The photo is opened by your browser on your own device to read its pixel size and draw the preview. Nothing is sent anywhere, and the page works offline once loaded.

Why can’t it open my HEIC photo?

Safari opens HEIC photos; some other browsers cannot. On iPhone, choosing a photo usually hands the page a JPEG copy. If a file will not open, type its pixel width and height instead: the result is the same.

Does printing at a higher DPI setting make the photo sharper?

No. The printer’s DPI is how finely it lays down ink. The detail comes from the photo’s pixels, so a low-PPI photo stays soft whatever the printer setting.
