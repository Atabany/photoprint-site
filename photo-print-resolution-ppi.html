Photo print resolution: pixels, PPI and cropping

By Mohamed Elatabany · Updated 3 October 2026

Print resolution is the number of image pixels per printed inch: PPI = visible pixels ÷ printed inches. For a 4 × 6 inch print at 300 PPI, use at least 1200 × 1800 pixels after the crop. 300 PPI is a useful target, not a guarantee of a good print.

Why cropping lowers resolution

Only the part of the source image that reaches the page counts. If a 1200 × 1800 image fills a 4 × 6 print, it supplies 300 PPI. If you zoom until only a 600 × 900 region remains visible at that same size, the print has 150 PPI. The original file’s total pixel count can therefore overstate the quality of a tightly cropped print.

How Photo Print describes quality

The app calculates PPI from the visible crop and the physical area the image occupies. Its labels are:

  • Excellent: 300 PPI or more.
  • Good: 200 to below 300 PPI.
  • Fair: 150 to below 200 PPI.
  • Low: below 150 PPI.

These are the app’s quality bands, not a passport authority’s rules. Focus, motion blur, lighting, compression, ink and paper affect the result too. A blurry original remains blurry even if it has many pixels.

PPI is different from printer DPI

PPI describes image pixels across the print. DPI describes the printer’s output dots. A printer’s advertised DPI does not replace the need for enough image detail. Changing a file’s resolution label without adding real detail does not make the original photo sharper.

If the quality warning appears

  1. Try the original camera photo rather than a screenshot or messaging copy.
  2. Reduce crop zoom if that preserves the composition.
  3. Choose a smaller print size if your use allows it.
  4. Print one test and assess it at the distance it will be viewed.
